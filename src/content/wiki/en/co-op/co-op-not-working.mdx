## First, the Two-Minute Checklist

Most Sephiria co-op problems are boring, and so are their fixes. Before trying anything elaborate: confirm every player is online in Steam (not offline, and not in an appear-offline state that quietly interferes with invites), confirm every player is on the same game version, restart the game, and send a fresh invite. This sequence resolves a large share of failed sessions on its own, and it costs two minutes.

If that does not clear it, work through the symptom table below, then read the section that matches your case. One piece of context explains a lot of the post-launch turbulence: Sephiria has patched frequently since the July 31, 2026 launch — version 1.0.25 and beyond arrived within the first month — and version mismatches between players are among the most common reasons a session refuses to start at all.

## Symptom-to-Fix Table

| Symptom | Likely Cause | First Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invite never arrives | Steam status or friends list state | Both players set Steam to Online; restart the Steam client; re-add the friend |
| Invite arrives but joining fails | Game version mismatch | Let Steam update both copies; confirm matching versions, then reinvite |
| Join hangs on loading | Session handshake failing | Host restarts the session; both players restart the game |
| Disconnects mid-run | Unstable connection between players | Prefer wired over Wi-Fi; restart routers if it recurs |
| Desync — enemies or items disagree between screens | Connection quality, or a rare post-patch bug | Regroup and stick together; if it recurs, verify files on both ends and check for known issues |
| Wrong-version or lobby error message | One player on an outdated patch | Update through Steam on every machine, then re-form the party |
| Fine solo, broken only in multiplayer | Firewall or NAT interfering with the session | Allow the game through Windows Firewall; test from another network |

## Invite and Connection Failures

When an invite does not land, the chain to check is short. Both players' Steam clients must be genuinely Online — if either side recently flipped to offline or appear-offline, invites can fail silently, so switch back and restart the Steam client, not just the game. If you became Steam friends only recently, try sending the invite in the other direction; in practice one direction often works when the other does not. And if the friends list itself is misbehaving — a not-uncommon Steam client hiccup independent of any game — restarting the client resolves that too. None of this is Sephiria-specific, which is exactly why it gets missed: people troubleshoot the game while the plumbing is the problem.

## Version Mismatches After Patches

A game that patches as often as Sephiria does guarantees version-skew sessions. Steam normally updates automatically, but a client that was in offline mode, a paused download, or a machine that slept mid-update leaves one player on yesterday's build while the rest are current — and multiplayer sessions between different versions fail. If any join attempt errors out with a version or lobby complaint, both host and joiner should exit the game entirely, let Steam finish updating, and confirm the same version before trying again. Check the official Discord to see what changed in the latest patch; the community surfaces co-op regressions within hours of an update, and knowing whether your problem is a known one saves an afternoon.

## Firewall and Network Settings

When the game runs fine solo but multiplayer misbehaves, look at the network path. Allow Sephiria through Windows Firewall — and through any third-party security software, which maintains its own rules — and prefer a wired connection over Wi-Fi for at least the host, because the host's connection stability is the ceiling on everyone else's experience. If problems persist across many sessions, testing from a different network (a phone hotspot is the classic experiment) tells you whether the issue lives inside your router setup or somewhere beyond it. Hosts on unstable links should consider handing hosting to the player with the steadiest connection and letting everyone else join them instead.

## Desync and Mid-Run Drops

Desync — two players seeing different enemies, positions, or item states — is the most disruptive co-op failure because it looks like the game is haunted rather than merely disconnected. Treat it as a connection-quality symptom first: regroup, keep the party physically close together in-game, and either finish carefully or end the run cleanly. If desync recurs across multiple sessions, verify game files on every affected machine, then check the official Discord for known issues on your exact version. The developers' frequent patch cadence cuts both ways here: updates occasionally introduce co-op regressions, but fixes ship quickly too, and pinning your report to a version number gets help faster.

## When to Verify Game Files

Verifying game files through Steam — right-click the game in your library, Properties, Installed Files, Verify integrity of game files — is the standard first aid whenever Sephiria behaves strangely after an update. Crashes on join, desync that survives every network check, and sessions that worked yesterday but fail today are all classic corrupted-or-half-patched-file symptoms. Verification takes a few minutes, repairs what it finds, and costs nothing. Do it before assuming the worst, and do it on every affected player's machine, not just the host's — a broken copy at any position at the table can destabilize the whole session.

## Escalation Order: What to Try, and When

If the symptom table feels like too much at once, work downward through this order. Each step is cheaper than the one below it, and most sessions are rescued in the first three.

| Order | Step | Time Cost | Do It When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Both players restart the game and send a fresh invite | Under two minutes | Always first |
| 2 | Confirm Steam Online status, restart the Steam client | Two minutes | Invites vanish or never arrive |
| 3 | Confirm matching game versions, update through Steam | Five minutes | Any version or lobby error |
| 4 | Verify integrity of game files on every machine | Five to ten minutes | Strange behavior after a patch |
| 5 | Firewall checks, wired connection, different-network test | Fifteen minutes | Solo works but multiplayer fails |
| 6 | Report to the official Discord with versions and error text | Ten minutes | Everything above failed |

## If Nothing Works

Some problems are not yours to fix. When every step here fails, gather the details that actually matter for a report — exact version numbers, whether solo play works, the precise error text, and what you already tried — and take them to the official Discord, which is the most up-to-date source and where the developers and community triage issues, or to r/sephiria. Check the latest patch notes before filing anything; with updates landing as often as they have since launch, your issue may already be fixed or already on the list for the next build.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does my friend's invite do nothing at all?**

Usually Steam status or version skew. Both players go Online in Steam, both restart the Steam client, both confirm the same game version, then send a fresh invite. The invite-direction swap — having the other player invite you — is worth one try before deeper troubleshooting.

**We played fine yesterday and cannot connect today. What changed?**

Almost certainly a patch. Frequent updates mean one of you may be on a different build after Steam updated overnight. Both players exit the game, let Steam finish updating, confirm matching versions, and try again.

**Does distance or region matter for co-op?**

Connection quality between players is what matters: long physical distances and unstable links raise the odds of drops and desync. Wired connections, a stable host, and avoiding Wi-Fi hotspots for hosting are the practical mitigations.

**Should both of us verify game files, or just the host?**

Both. A corrupted or half-updated copy on any player's machine can destabilize the entire session, and verification is quick — there is no reason not to run it everywhere before escalating.
