## What Is Sephiria?

Sephiria is a top-down action roguelite from TEAM HORAY, the studio behind Dungreed. You play as a bunny hero descending a dangerous tower to save the town of Bunnyville. Each run takes you floor by floor through combat encounters, leveling, loot decisions, and boss fights, and when you fall you carry persistent upgrades back with you so the next attempt starts stronger. Version 1.0 launched on Steam on July 31, 2026, and the game runs beautifully on Steam Deck if you prefer to play handheld.

The twist that sets Sephiria apart from other action roguelites is its inventory: a puzzle-like grid where the placement of your items matters as much as the items themselves. Learning that system early is the single biggest advantage a new player can give themselves, so this guide spends plenty of time on it.

## Controls and Setup for Your First Run

Sephiria uses a straightforward top-down action control scheme. You move with one stick or the keyboard, attack with a primary button, and map your dodge and skills within easy reach. Before your first descent, open the controls menu and confirm three things.

First, find your dodge or defensive action and commit it to muscle memory. Like Dungreed, Sephiria rewards players who avoid damage rather than tank it, especially in the early game before your build comes online. Second, locate your skill or talisman activation buttons, because popping cooldowns at the right moment swings fights. Third, check the pause or inventory shortcut so you can rearrange your grid mid-run without panic when you pick up something new.

Spend your first minute in the starting town instead of rushing the tower. Talk to the townsfolk, look at the shops, and get a feel for what the hub offers. The town is where your permanent progression lives between runs.

## Choosing Your First Weapon

Sephiria offers six base weapons: Sword and Shield, Greatsword, Dagger, Crossbow, Katana, and Quarterstaff (often called Staff). They play very differently from one another, and your first pick shapes how forgiving your early runs feel.

| Weapon | Playstyle | Beginner Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sword and Shield | Balanced melee with a defensive option | Excellent - safest way to learn enemy patterns |
| Greatsword | Slow, heavy swings with big impact | Very good - forgiving damage, community favorite |
| Dagger | Fast attacks, close range | Tricky - demands confident dodging |
| Crossbow | Ranged pressure from safety | Decent - good for cautious players |
| Katana | Precision melee with timing emphasis | Moderate - punishes sloppy positioning |
| Quarterstaff | Fluid melee with strong scaling | Strong - a top-tier meta pick at every level |

For a first run, Sword and Shield or Greatsword are the clearest recommendations. Sword and Shield lets you block or mitigate hits while you learn what each enemy telegraphs, which means more surviving, more learning, and more gold per run. Greatsword hits hard enough to end fights before they get messy, and it remains one of the strongest weapons in the game even at the highest difficulty tiers, so time invested learning it is never wasted. Save Dagger and Katana for later, when your reflexes and knowledge of enemy tells are sharper.

## The Inventory Grid: Sephiria's Core Puzzle

Every item you collect during a run lives on a grid, and adjacent placement creates synergy buffs through a mechanic the game calls adjacent slate reinforcement. In short: where you put an item changes what it does. Items that reinforce each other should sit next to each other, and a cluttered, scattered grid is meaningfully weaker than a tidy, deliberate one.

Treat the grid as a puzzle you are always solving. When you pick up a new talisman or artifact, do not just drop it into the first empty cell. Open the inventory, look at which existing items share synergies with the newcomer, and reshuffle. A five-minute habit of rearranging after every big pickup compounds into dramatically stronger runs.

A few practical rules for your first hours. Keep synergizing groups clustered together instead of spread across the grid. When two items fight for the same cells, favor the pair whose buff supports your weapon, for example attack reinforcement for Greatsword rather than something that does not apply. And when the grid gets full, prune weaker standalone items rather than breaking up a strong adjacent cluster.

## What to Prioritize on Each Floor

Each floor of the tower gives you limited time and attention, and beginners often waste both. A simple routine keeps your runs efficient.

| Priority | What to Do | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Clear enemies safely | Fight deliberately, dodge telegraphed attacks | Levels and gold fund everything else |
| 2. Check the grid | Place new loot with synergies in mind | Adjacent reinforcement is free power |
| 3. Visit NPCs | Talk to shopkeepers and quest-givers | Shops and quests give outsized value |
| 4. Grab quests | Accept any task before moving on | Free rewards for progress you already make |
| 5. Fight the boss prepared | Rearrange the grid and heal first | Bosses punish rushed, disorganized players |

Leveling matters because your level gates how much you can do with the grid and your gear in a given run. Gold matters because shops in the tower sell exactly the piece your build is missing. NPCs matter because some of them hand out quests whose rewards beat anything you would find on the floor itself. If you only remember one line from this table: never enter a boss room with an unsorted inventory.

## Common Newbie Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | What to Do Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ignoring item placement | You lose the adjacent slate reinforcement buffs entirely | Rearrange the grid after every significant pickup |
| Skipping NPCs and shops | Missed quests, gear, and unlock progress | Sweep every room and talk to everyone |
| Dying to greed | Chasing one more chest into a bad fight ends the run | Disengage when a fight turns against you |
| Ignoring talent points | Persistent upgrades make every future run stronger | Spend points between runs, every run |
| Weapon hopping every floor | You never learn any weapon deeply | Main one or two weapons for your first several runs |

The talent point mistake deserves a special callout. Talent points are persistent unlocks that carry across runs, unlike anything you find inside the tower. Beginners who leave them unspent are playing a permanently harder version of the game. After every run, whether it ended on floor three or at the final boss, spend what you earned before queueing the next descent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Sephiria friendly to genre newcomers?**
Yes. The moment-to-moment combat is readable, deaths feel fair, and persistent progression means even rough early runs move you forward. The inventory grid has depth, but its basics click within a run or two.

**What difficulty should a brand new player expect?**
The standard tower is tuned so that a first clear usually takes a number of attempts, each better than the last. Do not touch hard mode until after your first full tower clear; it is built for players who already understand builds.

**Which weapon should I actually start with?**
Sword and Shield if you want to learn defensively, Greatsword if you want fights to end quickly. Both remain viable forever, and Greatsword in particular sits at the top of the community meta alongside Quarterstaff.

**How long is a run?**
Run length varies with how thoroughly you explore, but expect a run to be a focused session rather than an all-day commitment, with the hub town and permanent upgrades giving you something to do between descents.
