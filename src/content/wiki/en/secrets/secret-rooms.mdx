## How Secret Rooms Work

Sephiria, the top-down action roguelite from TEAM HORAY — the studio behind Dungreed — hides more in its tower than most players ever see. Scattered through the floors are secret rooms: hidden chambers sealed behind wall segments that look almost identical to everything around them. The game never marks these rooms, never mentions them in its tutorials, and never prompts you when you pass one. Finding them is a learned skill, and it pays out on every single run once you have it.

The core mechanic is simple once you know it exists. Certain wall segments in a floor are destructible, and attacking them with a damaging hit — a basic attack, a skill, a projectile — breaks the section open and reveals a passage. Inside is a small hidden room containing rewards, and in a game where every descent compounds small advantages into stronger builds, checking suspicious walls is one of the cheapest edges available to you. This guide covers the cues that betray secret walls, the minimap as a second opinion, what to expect inside, and how to adapt the habit to each floor type.

## How to Spot a Candidate Wall

The single most important skill for finding sephiria secret rooms is recognizing when a wall segment looks wrong. The differences are deliberately subtle; these rooms were built for attentive players, not for everyone. Get into the rhythm of scanning the perimeter of each cleared room and every dead-end corridor for the cues in the table below.

| Cue | What It Looks Like | How Reliable It Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cracks | Thin fracture lines across one wall segment that neighboring tiles lack | High — the most commonly cited tell among players |
| Tile discoloration | A segment noticeably lighter or darker than the wall around it | Medium — depends on floor lighting and theme |
| Misaligned seams | Brick or panel lines that stop, shift, or fail to line up at one segment | Medium — easy to miss at speed |
| Unexplained dead ends | A corridor that ends at a flat wall with no architectural reason | High as a heuristic — always attack dead ends |
| Minimap oddities | A gap, notch, or pocket of space where you have not walked | High — see the minimap section below |

Two practical habits will do more for you than anything else in this guide. First, attack dead ends on sight: a wasted swing costs a fraction of a second, while a missed secret room costs everything inside it. Second, when a wall looks even slightly off, hit it anyway. Players arriving from Dungreed already know the contract: the community stress-tests every suspicious tile within days of launch, and it confirmed quickly that Sephiria's cracked and discolored walls are real and worth breaking.

## Reading the Minimap

The minimap is your most reliable second opinion, because a secret room is a real physical space and real spaces leave traces. Watch for two patterns. The first is the phantom gap: a notch or indentation in the geometry of an explored room, often along an outer wall, where no door is displayed. The second is the unreachable pocket: an area that reads as adjacent space but that you cannot actually reach through any visible opening. Either pattern suggests a hidden room bordering explored terrain.

When you spot a minimap oddity, cross-reference it with what you can see on screen. If the suspicious map area sits behind a wall segment that also shows a crack or discoloration, you have found your target — walk up and break it. One caveat worth knowing: fine details of map behavior have shifted across Sephiria's frequent patches. The game passed version 1.0.25 and beyond within its first month after the July 31, 2026 launch, and live games routinely tune how much hidden geometry the map reveals. For how the current build behaves, the official Discord is the most up-to-date source.

## What Rewards to Expect

Exact contents vary by run, floor depth, and patch, so treat this as qualitative guidance rather than a fixed loot table. What the community consistently reports is that secret rooms hold rewards worth the stop — pickups that keep a run's economy and gear options healthy while deeper floors eat into both. For verified, current reward pools and drop behavior, check Kean's Sephiria Compendium, the community Google Spreadsheet that serves as the standard data reference for the game.

| Reward Type | Why It Matters for Your Run |
| --- | --- |
| Currency and consumables | Free resources that compound across a full descent |
| Equipment and item opportunities | Extra chances at gear without spending gold in shops |
| Occasional rarer finds | The payoff that keeps veterans checking walls on every floor |

The correct mental model is not "a secret room will make my run" but "ten secret rooms per descent will make every run better." The value is in the aggregate, and the habit costs almost nothing once it is automatic.

## Per-Floor-Type Strategy

| Floor Context | What to Prioritize | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early floors | Sweep every dead end and cracked wall | Early pickups compound for the rest of the run |
| Shop and hub-like floors | Check walls adjacent to and behind structures | Reward rooms often border high-traffic areas |
| Puzzle and interactive floors | Solve the objective first, then sweep | Puzzles pull your attention away from the walls |
| Deeper tower floors | Spot-check only the highest-signal patterns | Time spent lingering in hostile floors is risk |
| Boss approach floors | One quick perimeter pass, then commit | The real reward on these floors is the boss door |

The unifying idea is that time is a resource in a roguelite. On early floors a thorough sweep is cheap insurance; on deeper floors, where enemies punish lingering, limit yourself to dead ends and confirmed minimap gaps and keep moving. A secret room you never reach is worth less than the run you lose trying to find it.

## A Per-Floor Routine That Scales

Clear the floor of enemies first — secret hunting with monsters on your back is how good runs end. Open the full minimap and look for gaps or pockets that do not match any door you have used. Attack every dead end you walked past. Then leave. The whole routine adds well under a minute per floor. Consistency beats obsessive wall-hugging: the players who find the most secret rooms are not the ones who tap every tile, they are the ones who never skip the routine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are secret room locations fixed between runs?**

The tower rearranges itself, so exact placements do not carry over between runs. Learn the cues — cracks, discoloration, dead ends, minimap gaps — and use the community maps to calibrate your eye rather than to memorize spots.

**Do I need a specific weapon or item to break a secret wall?**

No. The mechanic is simply attacking the suspicious segment; ordinary damage breaks it open. If a wall survives a few solid hits, it is scenery — move on to the next candidate.

**Do secret rooms count toward achievements or 100% completion?**

Exploration supports completion broadly, and constellation rooms in particular are directly tied to achievements and 100%. See our constellations and achievements guides for the full picture.

**Can I find secret rooms while playing co-op?**

Yes. The tower holds the same secrets in multiplayer, and any player in the party can break the wall. Assign one player to perimeter duty each floor so four people do not re-check the same dead ends.
