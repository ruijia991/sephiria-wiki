## How Sephiria Weapon Upgrades Work

In Sephiria, the action roguelite from TEAM HORAY that hit 1.0 on July 31, 2026, your weapon is not a fixed choice. Every run, the weapons you carry improve through upgrades offered as you descend the tower. These upgrades are the difference between a weapon that feels adequate in the early floors and one that shreds late-run content, and understanding the system is essential before you attempt hard mode, which scales to at least Tier 35.

Upgrades happen during the run itself. As you clear encounters and progress downward, you are offered chances to reinforce the weapon you are using: raw damage increases, attack speed improvements, wider or larger hitboxes, extra projectiles, and utility effects depending on the weapon. Because these offers are randomized, no two runs strengthen a weapon in quite the same way, and part of the skill of the game is deciding which offer to take now and which to skip in hopes of a better fit later.

The single most important principle: upgrade the weapon you actually intend to finish the run with. Spreading upgrades across multiple weapons during a single descent leaves all of them underpowered relative to the enemies' scaling. Commit early unless the run clearly forces a change.

## Evolution Branches: One Weapon, Multiple Destinations

The deeper layer of the system is weapon evolution. Certain weapons do not simply get stronger; at key points they can evolve into variant forms, and each variant points your build in a different direction. These branches are not reversible within a run, so the choice deserves real thought.

The classic example is the Flame Dagger. As it is upgraded during a run, it can evolve into one of two distinct forms:

| Evolution Path | Character | Best Suited For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blazing Blade | Emphasizes the blade side of the weapon's identity: stronger direct attacks | Aggressive playstyles that stay in melee range |
| Burning Fang | Emphasizes the burning side: damage-over-time and harassment | Safer playstyles that let effects do the work |

The Flame Dagger split illustrates the design logic behind every evolution in the game: each branch takes one facet of the base weapon and commits to it fully. Neither branch is strictly better. Blazing Blade rewards players confident enough to keep attacking; Burning Fang rewards patience and lets the burn chip enemies down while you focus on dodging. The right answer depends on your weapon comfort, your talismans, and what the run has offered so far.

Treat evolutions as build decisions, not weapon decisions. You are not choosing "which form is stronger" in the abstract; you are choosing which form fits the rest of your run.

## Choosing a Branch: A Practical Framework

When an evolution choice appears, run through these questions in order:

1. What does the rest of my build already do? If your talismans and artifacts already amplify direct strikes, a branch that boosts direct attacks compounds with them. If your setup favors sustained damage, a damage-over-time branch compounds instead.
2. How confident am I with this weapon? Safer, more passive branches cover weaknesses; aggressive branches multiply strengths.
3. How far into the run am I? Early branches shape everything that follows and deserve a conservative pick. Late branches can afford to be greedy, because you have less future to protect.
4. What is the run throwing at me? Boss-heavy remaining content favors burst windows; swarm-heavy content favors area coverage and sustained effects.

The framework matters more than any single answer. Because upgrade and evolution offerings are randomized per run, the strongest players are the ones who can re-evaluate quickly rather than follow a fixed script.

## Upgrade Priorities During a Run

When upgrade offers compete with each other, the following priority order is a dependable default for most weapons:

| Priority | What to Prioritize | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Damage output | Killing enemies faster is defense; short fights mean fewer chances to take hits |
| 2 | Attack speed or hitbox size | More consistent pressure and better crowd handling |
| 3 | Survivability and control effects | Freeze, knockback, and defensive layers keep bad situations recoverable |
| 4 | Utility and niche effects | Valuable, but situational depending on the run's remaining content |

Two exceptions worth noting. First, if your build is built around a control mechanic such as freezing, control upgrades can jump above raw damage, because the control itself is what keeps you alive. Second, in the earliest floors of a run, a damage upgrade now is usually worth more than gambling on a stronger offer later, since a weak early game is what ends most runs prematurely.

## Upgrades, Talismans, and the Inventory Grid

Sephiria weapon upgrades do not exist in isolation. Your full power comes from three interacting systems: the weapon's own upgrades, the talismans and artifacts you collect, and the puzzle-like inventory grid where you place them.

The inventory grid is where most of the optimization lives. Items placed in adjacent slots interact through the adjacent slate reinforcement mechanic: neighboring items reinforce each other, so clustering related talismans and artifacts produces synergies that scattered placement loses entirely. A weapon evolution branch that matches those clusters is dramatically stronger than one that fights them. Before committing to an evolution, glance at your grid: if your adjacency clusters already lean one direction, take the branch that leans the same way.

This is also why preset codes, the importable share codes that let players trade complete build configurations, usually encode more than just a weapon. They encode the whole arrangement, and copying a strong player's code teaches you both their branch choices and their grid layout at the same time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I evolve a weapon more than once in a single run?**

Evolution branches are committed choices within a run; once you take a branch, that run's weapon stays on it. Reaching a different branch requires a new run. Choose with that permanence in mind.

**Is Blazing Blade or Burning Fang better for the Flame Dagger?**

Neither is universally better. Blazing Blade suits aggressive players and builds that amplify direct attacks; Burning Fang suits safer playstyles and builds that amplify sustained damage. Match the branch to your talismans and confidence with the weapon, not to a fixed ranking.

**Should I upgrade two weapons in one run?**

Generally no. Upgrade offerings are limited, and splitting them leaves both weapons behind the enemy scaling. Commit to one main weapon and treat anything else as an emergency backup.

**Do weapon upgrades carry over between runs?**

No. Sephiria is a roguelite, and weapon upgrades and evolutions are per-run. Permanent progression lives elsewhere; every descent starts your weapon scaling from scratch.
